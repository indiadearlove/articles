Bad Data is Poison for AI - How can we keep it out?

By India Dearlove

28th March 2025

Chances are, you’ve contributed data that is now being used to train AI, whether by listening to music on Spotify, helping refine recommendation algorithms, or posting on Twitter or Reddit, where scraped content is used to teach AI how to mimic human conversations.

This is why understanding the role and impact of data is vital for everyone.

Building an AI: The Role of Data

Building an AI requires three key components: computing power, algorithms, and data. Of these, data is the only one that a broad range of people can influence. Training AI models demands vast amounts of high-quality data.

What is Bad Data?

Bad data can take many forms:

  • Inaccurate data – Errors from mistakes in data entry or misinformation.
  • Incomplete data – Missing key details that create gaps in understanding.
  • Outdated data – Information that is no longer relevant or applicable.
  • Biased data – Data skewed by human influence, intentionally or unintentionally, to produce certain results.

AI relies on data to learn, poor-quality data can lead to flawed decisions and unintended consequences.

What are the Consequences of Bad Data?

Since AI is being so widely used, the consequences of bad data can range from minor inconveniences to life-threatening mistakes.

Minor Inconveniences

At the smallest scale, outdated data can cause frustration. For instance Google Maps might have the wrong opening hours for a restaurant causing customer to try and go when closed. Read more

Outdated data on the latest trends might make it hard for an AI to recommend what will go viral on social media.

Reinforcing Bias and Discrimination

Bad data doesn’t just cause inconveniences—it can reinforce real-world biases. Apple’s credit card algorithm was found to exhibit gender bias because it was trained on historical financial data that reflected existing inequalities. As a result, women were given lower credit limits than men, even when they had identical credit histories and financial profiles. Read more

In Google’s photo labelling AI they misclassified black people as gorillas due to the data being used to train the AI featuring majority light skinned people. Read more

Medical AI Failures

IBM Watson for Oncology was meant to help doctors recommend cancer treatments, but it often gave inaccurate and unsafe advice. One reason for this failure was its reliance on synthetic data—artificially generated information used to supplement real-world data when there isn’t enough available. While synthetic data can be useful for training AI, if it doesn’t accurately reflect real medical cases, the AI can learn incorrect patterns and make dangerous mistakes. Read more

AI in Warfare

At its most dangerous, flawed data can cost lives. AI is increasingly used in warfare, including by the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) for target identification. Errors in data have led to misidentifications with potentially deadly consequences.

In one case, an AI system wrongly flagged high school students as militants after misinterpreting a list of those due to sit an exam. Fortunately, this mistake was caught in time.

In another, a woman named Hijazi was fleeing with her three daughters when their car was struck. While the exact cause is unclear, such incidents highlight the risks of AI making life-or-death decisions based on flawed or incomplete data.

How Can You Make a Difference?

You might be surprised to learn that even if you don’t actively use AI, you can still play a role in improving data standards. Here’s how:

1. Learn More About AI

Understanding AI is the first step to making informed decisions. If you're new to AI, this free course is a great place to start: The Future of AI by BlueDot.

2. Be Skeptical of AI-Generated Content

Avoid spreading misinformation. Misinformation spreads quickly, especially when AI is involved. Be cautious of AI-generated news, images, and deepfakes. Rely on reputable sources for information, and fact-check suspicious claims.

Did you know that engaging with clickbait boosts low-quality content? Algorithms on platforms like Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Google prioritize content based on engagement, meaning that every click, like, and share influences what AI promotes.

3. Encourage AI Literacy in Schools & Workplaces

AI isn’t going away, so the sooner people understand how it works, the better. Schools and workplaces should include AI literacy in education and training programs. Schools should have training for both students and teachers. Learn more about AI teacher training, here.

4. Provide Feedback on AI Systems

If you use AI tools like ChatGPT, giving feedback helps improve accuracy. When AI makes mistakes or provides biased responses, report them or downvote incorrect answers. Many platforms have built-in feedback options, using them helps train AI to be more reliable.

5. Demand Transparency & Higher Standards

While governments and companies set AI rules, public pressure can push for better data practices. The more people understand AI and its risks, the more pressure there is for transparency, fairness, and accuracy in AI. Read more about the UK's plans for the future of AI.

Final Thoughts

AI has the potential to be a powerful force for good, but only if it’s built the right way. Every step toward better data helps create AI that is more ethical, accurate, and beneficial for society.
